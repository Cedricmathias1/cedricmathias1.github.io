UX Research · Product Design

Unified Student Communication Hub

Reducing communication overload by 80% through human-centered design and strategic consolidation of academic platforms.

80% User Satisfaction
90% Ease of Use
8 User Interviews
5 Testers

Overview

Students are bombarded with messages from multiple platforms—Canvas, Gmail, WhatsApp, Teams—making it difficult to prioritize and manage academic and social communication effectively. This fragmentation leads to information overload, missed deadlines, and increased anxiety.

Challenge: How might we reduce cognitive load and help students manage academic communication more effectively across multiple platforms?

My Role

End-to-end UX research and design, from user interviews and persona development to wireframing, prototyping, and usability testing.

Timeline

12 weeks (September 2025 - December 2025) as part of UCC's Interaction Design course.

The Problem

Through initial research, I discovered that students face several critical pain points:

📱 Platform Fragmentation

Messages scattered across Canvas, Gmail, Teams, WhatsApp, and university portals.

🔔 Notification Overload

Duplicate notifications and constant interruptions cause stress and distraction.

⏰ Missed Information

Important deadlines and announcements get lost in the noise.

This problem affects nearly all students but is particularly challenging for international students communicating in a second language and students balancing work with studies.

Research Process

I conducted both primary and secondary research to understand the depth and breadth of this problem.

User Interviews

I conducted 8 semi-structured interviews with UCC students representing diverse backgrounds.

Key Research Findings

Communication Fatigue

Students check 4-6 different platforms daily, spending an average of 45 minutes just managing notifications.

Anxiety & FOMO

Fear of missing important information leads to compulsive checking and increased stress.

Lack of Prioritization

No clear way to distinguish urgent academic messages from general notifications.

Redundant Messages

Same announcements posted across multiple platforms create confusion.

Meet the Users

Based on interview patterns, I developed three primary personas:

👩‍🎓

Jenny

International Student

Anxious about missing information. Checks platforms excessively. Struggles with language barriers.

👨‍💼

Daniel

Working Student

Values efficiency. Frustrated by app switching. Wants task-focused communication.

👩‍💻

Aisling

Mature Student

Organized but overwhelmed. Prefers official channels. Mutes apps to protect mental health.

Design Solution

Based on research insights, I designed USCH with these core features:

📬 Unified Inbox

All academic messages in one place with smart filtering and categorization.

🎯 Priority System

Urgent, academic, and general categories with visual indicators.

🔕 Focus Mode

Filter out distractions and show only academic-critical information.

Design Process

Low-Fidelity Wireframes

Explored structure and functionality without visual design constraints.

Mid-Fidelity Prototypes

Refined hierarchy, spacing, and user flows based on feedback.

High-Fidelity Mockups

Applied visual design system and brand elements.

Usability Testing

Tested with 5 participants and iterated based on findings.

Results & Impact

Usability testing validated the design solution with strong positive results:

80% Intend to use regularly
90% Found it easy to use
0% Felt it was too complex
80% Felt confident using it

Key Learnings

Design Insights: Users preferred clean, minimal design over feature-heavy interfaces. Visual hierarchy and clear categorization reduced cognitive load significantly.

Process Learnings: User research validated every design decision. Iteration revealed new insights at each stage.
