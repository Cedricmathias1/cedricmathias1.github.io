Drip · Circular fashion concept

Good style. A longer story.

A service-design concept that connects shopping, product transparency, repair and resale.

Team project · 5 people · September–December 2025Research lead · Persona lead · Figma co-lead

Drip sustainability profile. A high-fidelity service concept, not a launched retail platform.

01 / The problem

Make the useful choice the sustainable one.

The brief asked our team to help a fast-fashion retailer explore circular services without abandoning its business model. Research pointed toward a practical tension: people value sustainability, but price, fit and convenience still shape the buying decision.

We designed around that tension instead of assuming more environmental messaging would change behaviour.

02 / My contribution

Research that changed the brief.

  • Research leadership. Led research into clothing behaviour and fashion waste during a 12-week sprint.
  • Persona development. Created Mary, a budget-conscious student whose impulse purchases helped focus the design on useful rewards and post-purchase options.
  • QR product passport. Championed a scannable product-information concept as a way to make material and origin claims more inspectable.
  • Figma co-leadership. Co-led the progression from independent sketches through a high-fidelity interactive prototype across five core screens.

03 / Solution structure

A garment has more than one chapter.

The concept connects a QR-based product passport with a wardrobe view, repair services, resale and rewards. A visible sustainability profile makes participation legible, while Eco Points and store credit give users a practical reason to return.

These are designed product flows. The prototype does not establish a production QR verification service, a working AI styling engine, or measured environmental savings.

04 / Outcome & learning

Prototype the service.
Then test the hardest moment.

The team delivered a high-fidelity interactive concept. My strongest learning was to design around the person who is not already convinced: affordability, trust and convenience need a place in the experience.

I would next test the repair-booking flow and product-information comprehension with users. I would also define a survey analysis framework before collecting responses, and map specific service moments rather than relying on a broad journey map.
